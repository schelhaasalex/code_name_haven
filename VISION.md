# Reclaim

**The people in the room matter more than the ones in your pocket.**

---

## What we believe

Phones aren't the problem. Being somewhere without being there is the problem.

Every product aimed at this treats the phone as an addiction and the user as
someone who can't be trusted: lock it in a box, block the apps, shame the
screen-time chart. That framing is adversarial, and adversarial products lose —
people uninstall the thing that nags them, and the ones who don't are the ones
who never needed it.

We think the actual want isn't less phone. It's more of the evening you were
already having. Nobody lies in bed wishing they'd scrolled less; they wish
dinner had gone on longer.

So Reclaim isn't restriction with a nicer font. It's a ritual, and the ritual
is social: one person sets their phone down, and everyone else's answers.

## The one thing that makes it work

Four other people watching you do it.

That's the mechanism, and it's why this is a different product from every
focus timer. A solo phone-free app is a promise you make to yourself, which is
worth roughly what those are usually worth. Five people declaring the same
evening at the same table is **mutual attestation** — nobody can fake four
friends, and nobody wants to be the one still holding theirs.

It needs no sensors, no blocking, no surveillance. The room corroborates
itself.

## What we're optimising

**Co-present evenings per group per week.**

Not hours. A household where four people dock separately on four floors scores
identically to one that ate dinner together, and only one of those is the
product. Hours reclaimed is a number we report; togetherness is the number we
care about.

And consistency over volume: four ordinary dinners beat one heroic Saturday.
No feature may let a long session outweigh showing up.

## What we won't build

**Nothing that punishes.** No decaying status, no streak you can lose, no
red badge counting your failures. A rhythm ends and yesterday still counts.
The moment this product can make you feel bad for not opening it, it has
become the thing it replaced.

**No leaderboards, ever.** The people you dock with see that you were there,
never how often you weren't. This is what makes it survivable with a
teenager — and a teenager who thinks the app is how their parents check on
them is a teenager who doesn't use it.

**No surveillance.** We never ask for your location. We can't see your screen,
your apps, or where you were. Not as a marketing position — as a consequence
of not needing any of it.

**No lock.** You can always pick your phone up. If the product only works
because you can't leave, it doesn't work.

## Where it goes

Places accrue. A table where this keeps happening becomes *a reclaim house* —
and that's the thing that spreads, because it's the host who invites people,
and the place that gets the credit.

The app is free. The business is a small object that sits by the door and
makes the gesture physical: you tap it on your way in, and the evening starts.
Everything in v1 — the printed card, the tag, the tap — is that object's
software ancestor, on purpose. If people won't tap a card, they'd never have
tapped the puck, and we'll have learned that for the price of some cardstock.

## How we'll know it's working

Not downloads. Not session length. Not daily actives — a product designed to be
used less cannot be measured by how often it's opened.

Someone says *"come over Thursday, we'll do the thing."*
