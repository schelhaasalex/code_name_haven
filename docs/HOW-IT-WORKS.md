# How it works

You set your phone down. The people around you do the same. The evening counts
itself, and the place you did it in slowly becomes something.

There is nothing to set up first.

---

## Starting an evening

Open the app and tap **Set it down**. That's it — it's already counting, and you
can put the phone face down, in a pocket, or on the table. Everything else here
is for when the phone is already out of your hands.

**Your usual place.** After a few evenings somewhere, the app offers it on the
home screen — one tap instead of two. It's a guess from your own history, not
from where you are.

**Ask.** "Hey Siri, set my phone down with Reclaim." Or "Reclaim this evening."

**Control Centre.** Add the toggle once, and starting an evening is a swipe and
a tap, without opening anything.

**The Action Button, or a shortcut.** The same action is available in the
Shortcuts app, so you can put it wherever suits you.

**A card on the table.** If someone has made a card for a place, touch your
phone to it. A banner appears; tapping it starts the evening at that place, and
you never have to find the app.

---

## Joining people

You never have to start the evening to be part of it.

**Somewhere you both know.** When someone sets theirs down at a place you share,
your phone says so once: *"Maya set theirs down at The Kitchen Table."* Join, or
don't. Nothing happens if you ignore it.

**A table next to you.** If a phone near you is already down, yours can notice
it and offer to join — no card, no link, nothing to scan. You'll be asked for
Bluetooth the first time you set your own phone down, and it's only ever used to
find the table in front of you.

**A link someone sent you.** Opening it makes you one of that place's people.
It does not start an evening — a message on a Tuesday afternoon isn't an
invitation to put your phone down right then.

---

## What counts

**Fifteen minutes.** Anything shorter is kept, but not counted as an evening.

**One a day.** An evening is a day, however many times you set your phone down
in it. Five people at one dinner is one evening each — not five.

**Alone counts.** Most evenings are alone, even for the most social person.
It's the same thing, smaller, and the app treats it exactly the same way.

**Three hours at most.** If you forget to pick it up, the evening is closed for
you and credited at three hours. It still counts.

---

## While it's running

Nothing is watching. The phone being face down is a nice gesture, not
evidence — in a pocket or face up and ignored counts exactly the same. No
sensor can start an evening, end one, or decide it didn't happen. If you tell
the app you were there, you were there.

The screen while an evening runs doesn't tick. It says when you started, and
shows the evenings you've had, with tonight's among them.

---

## Ending

Tap **End** — in the app, on the lock screen, in Control Centre, or by asking.
Ending never makes you unlock the phone.

Afterwards you get one screen: when it started, when it ended, how long, and
tonight's evening landing among the others.

---

## Places, and Somewhere

A place is just a name you gave a table. You don't need one — evenings without
a place are collected as **Somewhere**, and they count the same as any other.

When you do name one, every Somewhere evening you've had moves onto it. Nothing
is lost by having waited.

Each place gets a two-word handle, like *amber otter*, so a card or a link can
point at it without anybody having to type an address.

---

## What it will never do

- **Never show you what you missed.** No notification counts, no messages
  waiting, nothing about the time you were away.
- **Never name someone who hasn't set their phone down.** You'll see who
  arrived. You will never see who didn't.
- **Never track where you are.** It has no idea. A place is something you
  named, not somewhere it found you.
- **Never compare you to anyone.** Not to other people, not to other
  households, not to last week.

If it ever offers to count something for you — an evening it noticed you were
away from the phone, say — it asks, and it takes no for an answer.

---

## Reminders, if you want them

After a few evenings, the app may offer one nudge a day, around the time you
usually set your phone down. One, in the evening, never during. If you stop for
a while, they fade out on their own rather than getting louder, and you can turn
them off for good in Settings.
